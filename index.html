Axial dispersion encodes surface height.

  1. 01 / DISPERSE

    Wavelength-dependent focal depth

    The dispersive lens introduces controlled longitudinal chromatic aberration, focusing different wavelengths at different axial positions while limiting lateral chromatic aberration.

  2. 02 / CAPTURE

    Full-field optical acquisition

    The camera records the spatial distribution of the returned colour signal across the field of view. Axial information is encoded optically, without a mechanical Z-stack.

  3. 03 / RECONSTRUCT

    Calibrated surface reconstruction

    Colour-to-height calibration maps the recorded signal to axial position, producing a 3D topography for step-height and surface-profile analysis.

Research in progress / optical systems

Coherent diffraction imaging

A coherent probe illuminates overlapping sample regions. The detector records diffraction intensity; iterative phase retrieval uses these shared constraints to recover the object’s amplitude and phase.

A low-resolution starting estimate is updated from simulated diffraction intensities using a known probe. Repeated passes enforce consistency between overlapping regions and recover finer amplitude detail.

Product / A*STAR × JM Vistec

JMV-FLX 3D scanner

A configurable, high-speed fringe-projection 3D scanner developed with JM Vistec and Innovation Factory@SIMTech for automated inspection and machine-vision applications.

Structured-light fringe patterns are projected onto the object. Their deformation encodes surface geometry, which is reconstructed from camera images as a dense 3D point cloud. This enables non-contact, full-field surface profiling, with optical acquisition and 3D reconstruction configured to application requirements.
